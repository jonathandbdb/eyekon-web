Cataract surgery, explained simply

Cataract surgery is one of the most common and successful operations in the world. Here is what you should know.

What is a cataract?How does the surgery work?Which lens is right for me?Frequently asked questions

What is a cataract?

Inside your eye there is a natural lens that focuses light. With age it slowly becomes cloudy and yellowish: that is a cataract.

It happens to almost everyone eventually. It is not an illness you caused, and it can be treated.

  • Blurry or cloudy vision
  • Colors that look faded or yellow
  • Glare and halos around lights at night
  • Needing more light to read
CataractAfter surgery

How does the surgery work?

The cloudy lens is replaced by a clear artificial lens, called an intraocular lens (IOL). It stays in your eye for life.

Before

Your surgeon measures your eye and helps you choose the right lens for your lifestyle.

During

An outpatient procedure, usually under 30 minutes, with local anesthesia. You go home the same day.

After

Most people notice clearer vision within days. Follow your surgeon's drops and check-ups.

Which lens is right for me?

There is no single best lens — only the best lens for how you live. Talk about it with your surgeon.

Monofocal

Sharp vision at one distance, usually far. You may need glasses for reading.

Extended depth of focus (EDOF)

Good vision from far to intermediate — computer, cooking, dashboard.

Accommodative

Designed to move with the eye to help focus at different distances.

Natural Yellow

Filters part of the blue-violet light, like the natural lens of a young eye.

Frequently asked questions

Does cataract surgery hurt?

The eye is numbed with drops or local anesthesia. Most patients feel only light pressure.

How long does the lens last?

Intraocular lenses are designed to last a lifetime and do not need to be replaced.

Can both eyes be operated on at once?

Usually each eye is operated on separately, a few days or weeks apart. Your surgeon will decide.

Will I still need glasses?

It depends on the lens you choose. Many people need glasses only for certain tasks.

What are halos and glare?

Rings or glow around lights at night. Some lens designs cause more than others — the simulator helps you see the difference.
